    Questions

    What is photo metadata?

    Phones and cameras save extra information inside each picture: often the exact GPS position, the date and time, the device model and sometimes its serial number, editing software, and a small preview image. Most of it is invisible when you look at the photo, but anyone who gets the original file can read it.

    What does this tool remove, and what does it keep?

    It removes EXIF (including GPS), XMP, IPTC, comments, embedded previews, maker notes and any data appended after the image. It keeps only what's needed to show the picture correctly: the rotation flag and the colour profile. After cleaning, the file is scanned again and you'll see the result.

    Are my photos uploaded?

    No. All processing runs in JavaScript on your device. The page's security policy blocks all network connections, so it couldn't upload anything even if it tried. You can switch off Wi-Fi after the page has loaded and it will still work.

    Don't WhatsApp, Instagram etc. remove this anyway?

    Many social networks strip it from what other people see, but not every app or service does. Email attachments, cloud links, marketplaces, "send as file/document" options and many forums often pass on the original file unchanged. Cleaning it yourself means you don't have to rely on that.

    Which formats work?

    JPEG, PNG and WebP can be cleaned without any quality loss. Other formats your browser can display, like HEIC on some iPhones and Macs, AVIF or GIF, can be converted with “make smaller”, which also drops all metadata (GIFs lose their animation). Tip: on iPhone, the share sheet's “Options → Location” switch also removes the location.
